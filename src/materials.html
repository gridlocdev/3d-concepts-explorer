Materials

A material defines what a surface is made of: its color, how shiny it is, and whether it behaves like metal.

Most tools use physically based (PBR) materials, whose settings match real physical properties:

  • Base color: the surface color.
  • Metallic: 1 for metal, 0 for everything else. Rarely in between.
  • Roughness: 0 is a mirror, 1 is fully matte.
  • Specular: reflection strength on non-metals; around 0.5 for most.

Same sphere, different materials

The geometry is identical; only the material changes. Metals tint their reflections with their base color. Non-metals keep their color and get white highlights on top.

Some reference points: plastic is non-metal with medium to high roughness, polished wood is non-metal with low roughness, brushed aluminum is metal with medium roughness.
