Lighting

A 3D scene starts dark. Lights make it visible, and their type, color and position set the mood.

The same object under different lights, switching every few seconds: a warm point light, a cool directional light, and a hemisphere light that tints from above and below.

  • Point: shines in all directions from one spot, like a bulb. Fades with distance.
  • Directional (sun): parallel rays from one direction. No falloff.
  • Spot: a cone, like a flashlight. Set its angle and edge softness.
  • Area: light from a rectangle or disk. Soft, natural shadows.

Three-point lighting

  • Key light (top right): the brightest light, about 45° to the subject. It sets the main shadows.
  • Fill light (front left): 25 to 50% of the key. It lifts the shadows so they aren't black.
  • Rim light (behind): outlines the subject and separates it from the background.

The setup comes from photography and film, and works for product shots and character portraits alike. Large light sources give soft shadows; small or distant ones give hard shadows. Warm light feels cozy, cool light feels clinical.
