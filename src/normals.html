Normals

Every face has a normal: a direction pointing straight out of it. Renderers use normals to decide which side is the front and how light hits the surface.

A face has two sides, and most renderers draw only the front one. If a face's normal points inward, the face renders dark, black or not at all, and the model looks like it has holes.

To fix it, select the faces and use your tool's Flip Normals (or Reverse Normals) command, or recalculate all normals to point outward.

Flat vs smooth shading

Smooth shading adds no geometry. It blends the normals across each face so light changes gradually, which makes a low-poly surface look curved.

You can mix the two: smooth-shade an object and mark some edges as sharp. A phone, for example, has smooth sides and a hard edge around the screen.
