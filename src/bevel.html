Bevel

Overview

Bevel rounds off sharp edges to make things look more realistic - nothing in real life has perfectly sharp edges, so bevel makes your models look less 'CG'!

Understanding Beveling

Here's a crucial insight that separates amateur 3D work from professional: nothing in the real world has perfectly sharp edges. Even objects that appear sharp - a knife blade, a fresh sheet of metal, the corner of a building - have microscopic roundness when viewed up close.

Why this matters: Sharp edges don't catch light the way real objects do. When light hits a perfectly sharp edge in 3D, it creates an unnaturally harsh highlight. Beveling those edges - even slightly - allows light to play across the surface more naturally, immediately making your model look more realistic.

To bevel:

1. Switch to editing the mesh's components (vertices, edges, faces)
2. Select edges (or vertices)
3. Run the Bevel tool (some tools call it Chamfer)
4. Drag or type a value to set the width of the bevel
5. Raise the segment count for smoother rounding
6. Confirm

Hard Surface Modeling: Beveling is absolutely essential for hard surface work - mechanical objects, vehicles, architecture, product design. A good rule of thumb: if it would hurt to get hit by it in real life, it probably needs a bevel in your 3D model. Even subtle bevels (0.5-2mm on real-world scale objects) make a huge difference.

What You're Seeing

In the visualization at the start, compare the two cubes:

Orange cube (left): Has perfectly sharp edges. Notice how the highlights are harsh and thin, and the object has that telltale "CG" look.

Green cube (right): Has beveled edges. The rounded corners catch light across a wider area, creating more natural highlights and giving the object visual weight and realism.

The difference becomes even more pronounced under different lighting conditions. Beveled edges create subtle gradations in shading that make objects feel solid and tangible.

Bevel Width and Segments

In the visualization below, you can see three cubes demonstrating different bevel settings:

Left cube (orange): Small bevel width with 2 segments - creates a tight, subtle chamfer perfect for manufactured objects.

Middle cube (green): Medium bevel width with 3 segments - a balanced approach that works for most hard surface modeling.

Right cube (blue): Large bevel width with 5 segments - creates smooth, rounded edges ideal for worn or soft materials.

The segment count controls how smooth the bevel appears. More segments create a smoother curve but add geometry to your model.

How Much to Bevel

It depends on the material and wear. New, manufactured objects might have very subtle bevels. Worn or soft materials (wood, plastic) might have more pronounced rounding. Metal edges could be anywhere on this spectrum depending on the object's history.
