Shaders

A shader is a small program that runs on the graphics card and computes the color of each pixel of a surface.

Material settings like roughness and metallic are inputs to a shader. Writing or wiring your own shader lets you compute color from anything: position, angle, time. This torus knot is colored by sine waves of its surface position and the time, blended between two colors.

Most tools and engines have a node-based shader editor. Each node does one job (read the surface normal, sample a texture, mix two colors, do some math) and you connect them into a material.

Fresnel rim light

Surfaces reflect more when seen edge-on than face-on; that's the Fresnel effect, and it's why water looks brighter toward the horizon. This shader compares the view direction with the surface normal: where they're nearly perpendicular, at the sphere's edge, it adds a bright rim color.

The same trick gives force fields, holograms and character outlines. Other common shader effects: cel shading for a cartoon look, and procedural patterns that need no texture image.
