Camera

The camera is the viewpoint the final image is rendered from. You can look around the scene freely while working, but only the camera’s view ends up in the render.

The camera is an object like any other: move it, rotate it, animate it. Here it orbits the torus. Look through it in the viewport to see exactly what will render.

  • Focal length (mm) sets the field of view. Short lenses see wide, long lenses see narrow. 50mm is close to human vision.
  • Sensor size works with focal length to set the exact field of view. 36mm matches a full-frame camera.
  • Depth of field blurs what's out of focus. Set a focus distance and an f-stop; lower f-stops blur more.

Focal length

The view cycles through a wide, a normal and a telephoto lens, moving the camera back each time so the sphere stays about the same size. Watch the cubes behind it: the wide lens pushes them apart and exaggerates depth, the telephoto lens pulls them close and flattens it.

Wide lenses suit architecture and tight spaces; long lenses suit portraits and product shots. Low camera angles make a subject look imposing, high angles make it look small.
