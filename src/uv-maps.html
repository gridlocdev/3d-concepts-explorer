UV Maps

A UV map unfolds a 3D surface flat so a 2D image can be painted on it and wrapped back around the model.

U and V are the horizontal and vertical axes of the flat texture, named so they don't clash with X, Y and Z. Each vertex of the mesh gets a UV coordinate that says which point of the image it shows.

Notice the grid squares stretch and shrink across the sphere. A sphere can't be flattened without distortion; a good UV map keeps it small and puts it where it won't be seen.

Good vs poor unwrapping

Simple shapes can be unwrapped by projection. Complex ones need seams: edges you mark where the mesh is cut so it can unfold, like cutting open a box. Put seams where they're hidden, on undersides and along natural breaks.

UV maps are how logos, wood grain and painted detail land exactly where you want them, and most real-time engines texture models this way.
