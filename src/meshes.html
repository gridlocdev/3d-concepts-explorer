Meshes

Overview

A mesh is the skeleton of your 3D object - made of dots (vertices), lines (edges), and flat surfaces (faces) that create the shape.

Understanding Meshes

Almost everything you create in 3D modeling starts as a mesh - it's the fundamental building block of 3D modeling. Think of a mesh as the skeletal framework of your object, similar to how a building has a frame of steel beams before the walls are added.

A mesh is composed of three basic elements:

Vertices (dots): These are the corner points where edges meet. They're the fundamental building blocks - you can think of them as the joints in a skeleton.

Edges (lines): These connect vertices together, forming the outline of your shape. They're like the bones connecting the joints.

Faces (surfaces): These are flat surfaces bounded by edges. When you fill in the space between edges, you create a face - this is what becomes visible when you render your object.

Pro Tip: The complexity of your mesh directly impacts performance. A simple cube has just 8 vertices, but a detailed character model might have thousands. More detail means more realism, but also requires more computing power to manipulate and render.

What You're Seeing

In the visualization at the start, you can see the same object displayed three different ways:

Solid mesh (blue): This is what your finished object looks like with faces visible.

Wireframe (green): This shows only the edges, revealing the underlying structure of your mesh.

Vertices (red dots): These highlight just the vertices, showing you the control points of your mesh.

Mesh Complexity: Low-Poly vs High-Poly

In the visualization below, you can see how mesh complexity dramatically affects the final appearance:

Left (orange) - Low-Poly Sphere: Built with just 8 segments horizontally and vertically (320 triangles). You can clearly see the faceted structure. Perfect for game assets, stylized art, or when you need performance. Models render faster and are easier to edit, but curved surfaces appear angular.

Right (green) - High-Poly Sphere: Built with 32 segments (2,048 triangles). Creates a perfectly smooth sphere that's indistinguishable from a true mathematical sphere. Required for close-up renders and detailed sculpting, but requires more memory and processing power.

The key insight: more geometry isn't always better. Use only as much detail as you need for your specific purpose. A character's face needs high detail, but the back of their shoe doesn't. This is why game developers carefully budget polygons - placing detail where it matters most while keeping total counts manageable for real-time performance.

The Mesh as Foundation

Understanding that the mesh is just the shape - separate from its appearance - is crucial. You'll add materials, textures, and shaders on top of the mesh to give it color and surface properties like wood grain, metallic shine, or fabric texture.

The mesh defines the geometry and form. Everything else builds upon this foundation.
