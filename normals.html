Normals

Overview

Normals are invisible arrows pointing out from each face that tell Blender which side is the 'outside' - get them backwards and your model looks inside-out!

Understanding Normals

Normals are one of those concepts that seem invisible until something goes wrong - and then they become very obvious! Every face on your mesh has an invisible directional vector (think of it as an arrow) pointing perpendicular to its surface.

What normals do: They tell the rendering engine which side of a face is the "outside" and should be visible. This is crucial for how light interacts with your surface and which side actually gets rendered.

Think of a face like a piece of paper - it has two sides, but in 3D graphics, we typically only want to see one of them. The normal determines which side is considered the "front."

Common Problem: If your model appears dark, has black patches, or seems to have holes when it shouldn't, you likely have inverted normals. In Blender, select the problematic faces and use Alt+N → Flip to correct them.

What You're Seeing

In the visualization above, you'll see two cubes:

Left cube (green): Has correct normals pointing outward. Light bounces off it naturally, and all faces render as expected.

Right cube (orange): Has flipped normals pointing inward. Notice how it appears darker or may look like you're seeing the inside of the cube - because you are! The faces are being rendered from the "back" side.

Normals and Shading

Normals also affect smooth shading. When you smooth-shade an object, Blender interpolates the normals across faces to create the illusion of a curved surface. Understanding normals becomes essential when you want precise control over how light and shadow play across your model.
