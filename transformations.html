Transformations

Overview

The three basic moves in 3D: move (translate) things, rotate them, and scale them (make bigger or smaller). These are the fundamental operations you'll use constantly!

Understanding Transformations

Transformations are the bread and butter of 3D modeling. Every time you position an object in your scene or adjust its size, you're using one of these three fundamental operations.

Move (Translate): Changes an object's position in 3D space. The mathematical name is "translation"; tools call it Move, Translate or Grab. You drag the selection with the mouse or with a move gizmo (arrows along each axis) to reposition it.

Rotate: Spins objects around a pivot point. Rotating freely makes the object follow your mouse; the rotate gizmo's rings turn it around one axis at a time.

Scale: Makes objects larger or smaller relative to their pivot point. Scaling evenly keeps the proportions; scaling along one axis stretches or squashes the object.

Axis Constraints: Every modeling tool lets you constrain a transformation to a single axis - by dragging one handle of the gizmo, or with a modifier key. For example, constraining a move to the X-axis slides the object only left and right, however you move the mouse. This gives you precise control over transformations.

What You're Seeing

In the visualization at the start, you can see three cubes demonstrating each transformation:

Orange cube (left): Demonstrates Move by moving up and down along the Y-axis.

Blue cube (right): Illustrates Scale by growing and shrinking.

Axis-Constrained vs Free Transformations

In the visualization below, you can see how axis constraints provide precision:

Left (orange): Free rotation - the cube rotates on multiple axes simultaneously, creating complex motion. This gives you creative freedom but less predictability.

Middle (green): Y-axis constrained rotation - the cube only spins around its vertical axis, like a spinning top. Perfect for when you need controlled, predictable movement.

Right (blue): Z-axis constrained movement - the cube only moves up and down. Notice how constraining to a single axis makes the transformation precise and intentional.

Using axis constraints is essential for precision modeling. It prevents accidental movements and makes your workflow faster and more predictable.

Practical Application

These operations become muscle memory quickly. Beyond single axes, most tools also let you constrain to a plane - for example scaling only in the X-Y plane so an object gets wider without getting taller. Transformations apply not just to whole objects, but also to individual vertices, edges, and faces when you're editing a mesh's components.

Combined with axis constraints and numerical input (typing exact values for position, rotation or scale), transformations give you both intuitive manipulation and precise control over your 3D scene.
