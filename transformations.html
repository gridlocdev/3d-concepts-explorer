Transformations

Overview

The three basic moves in 3D: G to grab/move things, R to rotate them, and S to scale (make bigger or smaller). These are the fundamental operations you'll use constantly!

Understanding Transformations

Transformations are the bread and butter of 3D modeling. Every time you position an object in your scene or adjust its size, you're using one of these three fundamental operations.

Grab (G): Moves objects in 3D space. This is technically called "translation" in mathematics, but Blender calls it "Grab" to make it more intuitive. Press G and move your mouse to reposition the selected object.

Rotate (R): Spins objects around a pivot point. Press R to rotate freely, and the object will follow your mouse movement.

Scale (S): Makes objects larger or smaller. Press S and move your mouse away from the object to make it bigger, or toward it to make it smaller.

Axis Constraints: After pressing G, R, or S, you can press X, Y, or Z to constrain the transformation to a single axis. For example, G then X moves the object only along the X-axis. This gives you precise control over transformations.

What You're Seeing

In the visualization at the start, you can see three cubes demonstrating each transformation:

Orange cube (left): Demonstrates Grab by moving up and down along the Y-axis.

Green cube (center): Shows Rotation by continuously spinning.

Blue cube (right): Illustrates Scale by growing and shrinking.

Axis-Constrained vs Free Transformations

In the visualization below, you can see how axis constraints provide precision:

Left (orange): Free rotation - the cube rotates on multiple axes simultaneously, creating complex motion. This gives you creative freedom but less predictability.

Middle (green): Y-axis constrained rotation - the cube only spins around its vertical axis, like a spinning top. Perfect for when you need controlled, predictable movement.

Right (blue): Z-axis constrained movement - the cube only moves up and down. Notice how constraining to a single axis makes the transformation precise and intentional.

Using axis constraints (X, Y, or Z after pressing G, R, or S) is essential for precision modeling. It prevents accidental movements and makes your workflow faster and more predictable.

Practical Application

These operations become muscle memory quickly. You'll find yourself thinking "G-Z to move it up" or "S-Shift-Z to scale only in the X-Y plane" without conscious effort. They're applicable not just to objects, but also to individual vertices, edges, and faces in Edit Mode.

Combined with axis constraints and numerical input (you can type exact values after pressing the hotkey), transformations give you both intuitive manipulation and precise control over your 3D scene.
